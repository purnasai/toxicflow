## Findings

- Earlier-session recall for `dispatcher`, `notifier`, `records`, and `web` 
returned no findings or fixes.
- The per-agent scan found no agent toxic on its own: it saw `web` with 
`untrusted_input`, `records` and `notifier` with `private_read`, and `notifier` 
with `exfil_sink`. The multi-hop search found **two toxic flows**; neither was 
`UNKNOWN`.
- Most dangerous path: `web` reads untrusted content via `github.get_issue` → 
reports to `dispatcher` → `dispatcher` instructs `notifier` → `notifier` reads 
private `memory/notes` via `memory.read_graph` and can send data through 
`slack.slack_post_message`.
- The other flow starts at `web` and reaches `records` via `dispatcher`; 
`records` can read private `filesystem/records` via `filesystem.read_text_file`,
with the path continuing through `dispatcher` to `notifier` and its Slack sink.

## Repair

No available repair was found that removes every flow while keeping every task 
working. `search_repairs` returned no viable sets. The obvious single fixes are 
insufficient or break tasks: typed-only returns leave flows; revoking required 
read, input, or sink capabilities breaks tasks; and cutting required delegations
breaks tasks without necessarily eliminating the shared-store route.

I simulated a containment attempt—isolating `web` from `memory/notes` and 
quarantining the `dispatcher` delegations to `web`, `records`, and `notifier`. 
It preserved tasks but **left both flows**. I called `propose_repair` once with 
those actions; it was rejected by simulation. No repair was approved.
